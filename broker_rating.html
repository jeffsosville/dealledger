Editor's Note

Same listings, different reality.

For forty years, the lower middle market business brokerage industry has operated without a public reputation layer. EDGAR exists for public companies. Carfax exists for used cars. Zillow shows days-on-market for residential real estate. Main Street has had nothing.

DealLedger publishes broker behavior derived from public BBS listing data. Every active listing is matched to a canonical broker. Days-on-market is computed from estimated listed dates. Listings over one year — what the data calls "stale inventory" — become a measurable signal of operational discipline rather than an opinion.

The findings, on first analysis: operational practices vary by 30× within the same industry, the same brand, and sometimes the same city. A handful of brokers run books of 200+ active listings with zero stale inventory. Others maintain inventories where every listing has been on the market for over a year. Same business. Different choices.

Finding I · The Disciplined Books

Some firms run spotless books.

Across 200+ verified brokers, a small group maintains zero stale inventory — no listing on their book has been actively marketed for more than a year. This is not luck. It reflects active inventory management: withdrawing listings that aren't selling, declining mandates the broker can't actually move, and refusing to use stale listings as SEO bait.

Finding II · The Stale Inventory

Other firms maintain portfolios of year-plus listings.

At the opposite end of the dataset, several brokers maintain books where the majority — and in one case, all — of their listings have been on the market for over 365 days. The longest-marketed listings in the dataset go back to 2021. This pattern raises questions about whether listings are actively being marketed or whether they're maintained for SEO and lead capture after the underlying mandate has gone cold.

Finding III · The Franchise Variance

Same brand. Same playbook. Different reality.

Multi-office franchise systems show enormous variance in operational discipline across their offices. Buyers reading a Transworld listing in one city are evaluating an entirely different operation than buyers reading a Transworld listing in another. The brand sign on the door means very little about how a particular office runs its book.

Methodology

How we approach the data.

DealLedger is a publicly-derived dataset. Every number on this page is computed from public BBS listings. No proprietary feeds. No paid data partnerships. No special access.

Right to respond
Any broker on this list who believes their fingerprint is inaccurate can submit a correction. Corrections are published alongside the original metric. Methodology updates are versioned. EDGAR posture: we publish what's true and let people respond to it, rather than removing data because it's uncomfortable.
