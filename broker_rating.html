Overview

Same business. Different reality.

For decades, the lower middle market business brokerage industry has operated without a public reputation layer. EDGAR exists for public companies. Carfax exists for used cars. Zillow shows days-on-market for residential real estate. Main Street brokerage has had nothing.

DealLedger publishes broker behavior derived from public BBS data. Active listings are matched to canonical brokers via account ID. Lifetime sold counts are tracked over time to compute 12-month BBS-recorded velocity. Brokers are rolled up to the firm level by URL or normalized firm name.

The findings, on first analysis: BBS-recorded transaction patterns vary by orders of magnitude within the same industry, the same brand, and sometimes the same city. Some firms close 400+ deals in 12 months on BBS. Others maintain hundreds of active listings with no BBS-recorded closes at all. Whether that reflects actual deal flow or BBS-maintenance practice is a question we surface — not one we answer.

Inventory Without Recorded Closes

Firms with 50+ active listings and no BBS-recorded closes in 12 months.

These firms maintain substantial active inventory on BBS, but their public BBS sold-listing counts did not increase between April 2025 and April 2026. This is an observation about BBS metadata, not a claim about transaction activity.

A firm appearing here may genuinely have low transaction volume — or may be closing deals that aren't being marked sold on BBS. Brokers update their sold-listing counts manually; many list to capture buyer leads but never update status when deals close off-platform. The lifetime sold count for some firms here suggests historical activity that simply isn't being maintained currently.

Each firm on this list can verify or correct their own data. If your firm is listed here and you've closed deals in the past 12 months that aren't reflected on BBS, that's the gap between what BBS captures and what's actually happening in lower middle market deal flow. We'd note that as the finding, not the firm.

Methodology

How the data was built.

DealLedger is publicly derived. Every number on this page is computed from public BBS broker profiles and listing pages. No proprietary feeds, no paid partnerships, no special access.

What we measure
This page shows what BBS publicly displays for each firm: their active listing count, their lifetime sold count, and the change in that sold count between April 2025 and April 2026 (which we call "BBS Closes"). All three are broker-maintained on BBS — they reflect what the broker chose to display, not externally verified transaction data.
What we don't measure
Closes that happened off-BBS, deals that closed but weren't marked sold by the broker, listings that were withdrawn rather than sold, or transactions captured in other listing services. A firm with "0 BBS closes" may still be transacting actively; we have no way to know.
Right to respond
Any firm on this list can submit a correction. Corrections are published alongside the original metric. Methodology updates are versioned. EDGAR posture: we publish what's true about public BBS data and let firms respond, rather than removing data because it's uncomfortable.
Coming next
Listing-level detail per firm (median DOM, % over 1 year, asking price distribution); embeddable broker fingerprint cards; office-affiliation refinements for multi-state franchise rollups; quarterly velocity refresh.
